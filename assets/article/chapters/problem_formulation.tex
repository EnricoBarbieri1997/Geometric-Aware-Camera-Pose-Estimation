\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}

We consider a 3D scene containing $M$ cylinders $\mathcal{C}_j$ in general positions, each with known radius, position, and axis direction $\mathbf{w}_j$. All the metrics can be provided both in absolute value, and in this case the reconstruction will be metric, or relative to each other, and in this case the reconstruction will be up to scale.

A camera with unknown but fixed intrinsic matrix $\KK$ captures $N$ images $\{I_1, \ldots, I_N\}$ of the scene. Each image $I_i$ is associated with an unknown camera matrix $\PP_i = \KK[\RR_i \,|\, \mathbf{t}_i]$, where $\RR_i$ and $\mathbf{t}_i$ denote the rotation and translation of the camera in view $i$.

In each view $I_i$, each cylinder $\mathcal{C}_j$ projects onto a pair of \emph{contour lines}, denoted $l^h_{ij}$ with $h = 1, 2$ (see Figure~\ref{fig:problem_formulation} that displays one of the two silhouette lines of $\mathcal{C}_j$). These lines correspond to the visible contours of the cylindrical surface. Geometrically, each $l^h_{ij}$ arises from the intersection between the image plane and the planes $\Pi^h_{ij}$ that pass through the optical center of the camera $\PP_i$ and are tangent to the surface of $\mathcal{C}_j$.

The problem can be posed under flexible assumptions on the intrinsic parameters of the camera, such as zero skew, known aspect ratio, or a centered principal point. The number $M$ of cylinders and the number $N$ of images vary accordingly.

\paragraph{Output}
Given in input the parameters of the 3D cylinders $\{\mathcal{C}_j\}_{j=1}^M$ and a collection of $V$ silhouette lines $\{l^h_{ij}\}$ observed in the images, our aim is  twofold:\\
\textbf{1. Camera calibration:} estimate the unknown intrinisc parameters of the calibration matrix
\begin{equation}
\KK=\begin{pmatrix}f_x & s &c_x\\ 0 & f_y & c_y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
that correspond to  the focal lengths $f_x, f_y$, the skew $s$, and the coordinates of the principal point $(c_x, c_y)$.
Depending on the assumptions made on the intrinsic parameters, different variants of the problem can be considered. By introducing suitable linear constraints -- such as zero skew ($s = 0$), known aspect ratio ($f_x = \alpha f_y$), or a centered principal point -- the original set of five unknowns can be reduced. \\
\textbf{2. Pose estimation:} estimate the extrinsic parameters, namely rotations $\RR_i$ and translations $\mathbf{t}_i$, for each image $I_i$.
