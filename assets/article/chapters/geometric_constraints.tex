\subsection{Geometric constraints on cones and  silhouette lines}
\label{sec:constraints}

Following~\cite{gummeson22pose, gummeson24relative}, we will now describe geometric properties of cylinders and their image projections, which form the basis for the constraints in our systems.

In three dimensioin projective space $\mathbb{P}^3$, a cylinder $\QQ$ is defined as a set of points $\mathbf{X} = (x, y, z, 1)^\top$ such that the following equation holds:
\begin{equation}
	\mathbf{X}^\top \QQ \mathbf{X} = 0,
\end{equation}

If $\QQ$ is full rank, this defines a proper quadric surface, if instead $\QQ$ is rank deficient, it defines a degenerate quadric surface, the most common examples are cones, and cylinders.

Rather than working directly with $\QQ$, it is convenient to use the dual quadric $\QQ^* = \QQ^{-1}$, which encodes the set of all planes tangent to $\QQ$:
\begin{equation}
	\Pi^\top \QQ^* \Pi = 0,
\end{equation}
See Figure~\ref{fig:quadric_surface_dual}.

Even tho for a degenerate quadric the matrix $\QQ$ is rank deficient and $\QQ^{-1}$ is not defined we can still formulate the dual for degenerate quadrics directly in terms of the null-space of $\QQ$. 

\paragraph{}A cylinder $\mathcal{C}$ can be interpreted as a cone with its vertex $\mathbf{V}$ lying on the plane at infinity. Algebraically, both cones and cylinders are represented by a singular quadric surface with matrix $\QQ$ such that a point $\mathbf{X}$ belongs to $\mathcal{C}$ if and only if $\mathbf{X}^\top \QQ \mathbf{X} = 0$.

A cylinder is a generate quadric and as such the rank of it's matrix $\QQ$ is not full. The vertex $\mathbf{V}$ lies in the null-space of $\QQ$, that is, $\QQ \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{0}$. For cylinders, this vertex takes the form $\mathbf{V} = {(\mathbf{w}, 0)}^\top$ in homogeneous coordinates, where $\mathbf{w}$ defines the direction of the cylinder’s axis.

Technically speaking, all planes containing $\mathbf{V}$ are tangent to $\QQ$ but not all of them uniquely define the surface $\QQ^*$. See Figure~\ref{fig:cone_plane_singlepoint}.
To uniquely identify the dual quadric $\QQ^*$ in terms of the tangent planes two conditions must be satisfied:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{cases}
		\Pi^\top \QQ^* \Pi = 0, \\
		\Pi^\top \mathbf{V} = 0.
	\end{cases}
\end{equation}

These two conditions can be formulated in terms of silhouette lines $l^h$. The backprojection of each silhouette line, given by $\PP^\top l^h$, defines a 3D plane that is tangent to the cylinder $\mathcal{C}$ and passes through the camera center. Moreover, since this tangent plane is  aligned with the axis of the cylinder, $\PP^\top l^h$ must contain the point at infinity $\mathbf{V}$ corresponding to the direction of the cylinder's axis. 
As a result, each visible silhouette line $l^h$ induces two constraints on the camera parameters of the form:

\begin{align}
	l^{h \top} \PP \QQ^* \PP^\top l^h & = 0 \label{eq:dual_quadric_constraint} \\
	l^{h \top} \PP \mathbf{V}         & = 0.\label{eq:vertex_constraint}
\end{align}
