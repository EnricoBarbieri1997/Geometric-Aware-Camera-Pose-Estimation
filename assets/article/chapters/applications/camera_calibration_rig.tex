\subsection{Camera Calibration Rig}
\label{sec:camera_calibration_rig}

% What is the problem?
Camera calibration is a crucial step in computer vision applications, ensuring that the camera's intrinsic and extrinsic parameters are accurately determined. Traditional methods often require simple but manual steps to achieve, and can be time-consuming. For example the classical checkerboard calibration requires printing a pattern, cutting it out, and securing it to a flat surface. This process can be cumbersome, especially if the camera is highly angled with the planer surface.

% How does the proposed method solve it?
The proposed method introduces a 3D printable calibration rig composed of simple cylindrical objects. The symmetry of the rig allows for easy placement and orientation, reducing setup complexity. Unlike planar patterns, the cylindrical design enables robust feature detection from a wide range of viewing angles, making the calibration process more flexible and less sensitive to camera positioning. This approach streamlines the calibration workflow and improves usability in practical scenarios.
The calibration rig 3D model is available at \href{https://github.com/EnricoBarbieri1997/cylinder-based-homotopy-camera-calibration/assets/calibration_rig.stl}{calibration rig}.

\paragraph{Procedure}
3D print the calibration rig (Figure~\ref{fig:camera_rig_3dprint}), position it against a high contrast background (Figure~\ref{fig:camera_rig_setup}), and take two photos of the rig from different angles (Figure~\ref{fig:camera_rig_photo}).
The photos should be taken as close as possible to the rig, ideally capturing the cylinders extending beyond the edges of the shot to enhance accuracy.
Next, run each photo through an edge detection algorithm (Figure~\ref{fig:camera_rig_edge_detector}), such as Canny edge detection, Hough Transform, RANSAC, to identify the edges of the cylinders.
Manually match the detected edges to each cylindrical object in the rig (Figure~\ref{fig:camera_rig_match}).
Automatic matching is possible if the camera shots are claer enough of other items and has been lightly tested. Fully exploring this task is beyond the scope of this paper and the collected amount of data is not enough to support or reccomend any particular method.
Finally, run the homotopy algorithm to estimate the camera parameters (Figure~\ref{fig:camera_rig_homotopy}).
